Elmiron Field Phase 4 · Coaching & the console

Coaching, or a file being kept on you.

Identical information, two completely different products, and the difference is entirely design. Six screens: the MR's feed, an analysis with its citations, a reply — then the manager's queue, the review with its override, and admin.

Console density

Manager and admin run the same Phase 1 tokens at compact density — a desk product on a laptop, not a phone in the sun. Same palette, tighter rhythm, 13–15px base.

You see it first

Stated on the feed, not buried in a policy.

Every finding cites

Timestamped span, tappable, audio while it exists.

Reply on the card

Not in a menu, not on another screen.

Two worked, one to try

Never a list of six failures.

No score anywhere

No composite, no rank, no percentile. Your own past only.

Admin gets clarity and nothing else — plain tables, no charts, no dashboard. The one place it earns design attention is consent versioning, because "which notice did this doctor actually see" is the question that gets asked in a dispute.

The five rules, and where each one lands
1 · MR first, and the UI says so

Top of the feed on D1, footer of D2, and in the manager's own review panel on E2 — where it is a statement of fact, not reassurance.

2 · Every finding cites

Timestamp, quoted span, play control while audio survives. On E2 the manager sees the rep's next line too, so the citation cannot be read out of context.

3 · Reply on the card

A button on the feed card itself, a full screen behind it with voice input and drafts. Reply status appears in the manager's queue table.

4 · Two worked, one to try

D2 is structured as two sections with those exact headings. There is no room in the layout for a sixth failure.

5 · No score

The only number on the MR's feed is a count of objections answered against their own previous months. The manager has no team ranking view because none exists.

One thing I added that isn't in the brief

"3 of 42 reviewed." The brief doesn't ask for it, and showing how little is looked at might feel like it weakens the product. It does the opposite — an MR who believes every visit is analysed behaves like someone under total observation. The ratio is the difference between sampling and surveillance, and it is true, so say it.
